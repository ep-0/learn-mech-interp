## Why this article exists

Every activation, embedding, and feature direction in this curriculum is a vector in $\mathbb{R}^n$, and nearly every claim about what a model represents is a claim about spans, subspaces, and independence. This article establishes that vocabulary.

## What this article will cover

- Vectors as points and as directions, and why both readings are used
- Addition and scalar multiplication, and what closure under them buys
- Span, linear independence, basis, and dimension
- Subspaces, and the sense in which a feature can occupy one
- Why a vector has no coordinates until a basis is chosen
