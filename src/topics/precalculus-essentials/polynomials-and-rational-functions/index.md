## Why this article exists

Two things downstream need this specifically: the characteristic polynomial whose roots are a matrix's eigenvalues, and the polynomial approximations that Taylor expansion produces. Rational functions matter because indeterminate forms are where limits get interesting.

## Required sections

The finished article must cover the following, in this order. Headings can be reworded; the content cannot be dropped.

**1. Polynomials**

- Degree, coefficients, and leading behavior
- Roots, factoring, and the relationship between the two
- The fundamental theorem of algebra, stated, with the observation that some roots are complex

**2. Working with roots**

- The quadratic formula and the discriminant's three cases
- Why a real polynomial can have no real roots, with $x^2 + 1$ as the example that motivates complex numbers
- Multiplicity, and what a repeated root looks like on a graph

**3. Rational functions**

- Ratios of polynomials, and the domain exclusions they force
- Vertical asymptotes, holes, and end behavior
- Simplifying a ratio that is $0/0$ at a point, which is the algebra every derivative computation needs

**4. Polynomials as approximations**

- Fitting a line and then a parabola through a point, informally
- Why higher degree buys local accuracy, previewing Taylor series
- The characteristic polynomial named, so it is familiar when eigenvalues arrive

## Deliberately out of scope

A standard course covers these. This one does not, because nothing downstream needs them.

- Polynomial long division beyond simplifying a rational expression
- Partial fractions, synthetic division, and the rational root theorem
- Graphing techniques for their own sake

## What you should be able to do afterward

If any of these is still out of reach, the article is not finished.

- Factor a quadratic and state its roots, including when they are complex
- Simplify a rational expression that is indeterminate at a point
- Explain why a matrix with real entries can have complex eigenvalues, in terms of a polynomial

## Sources to learn from

- Axler, *Precalculus: A Prelude to Calculus*, chapter 2 — Start here. Polynomials and rational functions, with the algebra done cleanly.
- Simmons, *Precalculus Mathematics in a Nutshell*, the algebra section — Short and targeted at what calculus actually uses.
- Paul's Online Math Notes, 'Polynomial Functions' — Free, with the graphing and root-finding exercises.
- OpenStax, *Precalculus*, chapter 5 — Reference for anything the shorter sources leave out.
