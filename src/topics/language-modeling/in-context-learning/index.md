## Why this article exists

In-context learning is the behavior that induction heads and function vectors offer mechanistic accounts of, and those accounts are only assessable once the behavior itself is pinned down.

## What this article will cover

- Zero-shot, few-shot, and the demonstration format
- How in-context performance scales with model size and example count
- What the demonstrations supply: the mapping, the label space, or the format
- Induction as pattern completion, described behaviorally
- Task vectors as a behavioral observation, before any mechanism is proposed
